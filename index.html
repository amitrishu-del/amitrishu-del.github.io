Where power electronics
meets plasma physics

I trust a design once I can simulate it.— why I wrote Xcircuitsim

At Lam Research I develop RF technology for next-generation etch equipment. Much of my recent work is about shaping the voltage at the wafer: controlling ion energy with pulsed, non-sinusoidal waveforms, and recovering energy that would otherwise be lost. Most of my patent filings are in this area.

I came to plasma from power electronics. I started at DRDO in 2009, designing high-voltage converters and modulators for microwave transmitters. My PhD at NUS, on converters for more-electric aircraft, was published by Springer as an outstanding thesis. I then designed RF generators and matching networks at Advanced Energy while teaching power electronics at NUS, and joined Lam in 2022, working in Singapore, California and now Bengaluru.

That habit of simulating first is why I ended up writing my own circuit simulator, Xcircuitsim. The games are for my son, who is four and a half. I build each one around what he needs and what he is curious about at the time.
